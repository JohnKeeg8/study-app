# Physical security

Physical access can bypass logical controls. An attacker at the console can reset passwords, plug in rogue devices, or steal equipment.

## Cameras

- **Purpose:** **deter** (visible cameras) and **detect and record** evidence for investigations.
- **Types:**
  - **PTZ** (pan-tilt-zoom) cameras can be steered to follow activity.
  - **Infrared** cameras record in low light.
  - Motion-triggered recording saves storage.
- **Placement:** cover **entrances, server rooms, aisles, and rack rows** with no blind spots, and protect and retain the recordings.

## Locks and entry control

| Control | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Badge / proximity card reader** | Something you have. Logs every entry; lost badges can be disabled instantly |
| **Biometric lock** | Fingerprint, face, or iris; tied to the person, not a shareable credential |
| **Cipher lock** | Keypad code. Codes can be shared or observed, so rotate them |
| **Traditional keyed lock** | Simple, but lost keys mean re-keying |
| **Multifactor door access** | Badge + PIN, or badge + biometric |
| **Locking racks and cabinets** | Protect devices and console ports in shared spaces |
| **Cable locks** | Secure laptops to desks |

## Preventing tailgating and other risks

- **Access control vestibule** (formerly "mantrap"): two interlocking doors, only one open at a time.
- **Visitor logs and escorts** for people in sensitive areas.
- **Security awareness**, so staff don't hold doors open.
- **Alarm sensors** (door contacts, motion) for after-hours entry.
- **Bollards** stop vehicles; fences, lighting, and signs deter.
- **Tamper-evident seals** show whether equipment was opened.
- **Privacy screens** and a **clean desk policy** help against shoulder surfing and exposed documents.
- **Faraday cages** block electromagnetic signals.

> **Exam tip:** "Stop someone following an employee through a door" → access control vestibule. "Revocable entry with an audit trail" → badge reader. Cameras both **deter** and **detect**.
