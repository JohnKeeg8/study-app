# Disaster recovery

## The four metrics

| Metric | Meaning | Lower or higher is better? |
|---|---|---|
| **RPO** (recovery point objective) | Maximum acceptable **data loss**, measured in time back to the last good copy | Lower. Driven by backup and replication frequency |
| **RTO** (recovery time objective) | Maximum acceptable **downtime** before service must be restored | Lower. Driven by the recovery method and site type |
| **MTTR** (mean time to repair) | Average time to fix a failure | Lower. Spares, documentation, and support contracts help |
| **MTBF** (mean time between failures) | Average uptime between failures | **Higher** = more reliable |

Example: backups every 6 hours → worst case you lose **6 hours** of data (RPO). Near-zero RPO needs **synchronous replication**.

## Recovery sites

| Site | Ready state | Recovery time | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hot** | Fully equipped with current data | Minutes | Highest |
| **Warm** | Hardware in place; data must be restored | Hours to days | Medium |
| **Cold** | Space, power, and cooling only | Days to weeks | Lowest |

Cloud **DRaaS** can provide hot or warm capacity without a second data center. Keep sites far enough apart (**geographic diversity**) and use **diverse ISPs**.

## High availability designs

- **Active-active:** all nodes carry traffic at once. Each must stay under about 50% load so one can absorb the other's traffic if a node fails.
- **Active-passive:** a standby node takes over on failure. Simpler, but idle capacity.
- **Redundancy:**
  - **N+1** means one more component than needed.
  - Redundant power supplies, links, and **clustering**.
  - Remove **single points of failure**.

## Backups

- **Full:** everything.
- **Incremental:** changes since the **last backup of any type**. Fast to back up; a restore needs the full plus every incremental.
- **Differential:** changes since the **last full**. A restore needs the full plus the latest differential.
- **3-2-1 rule:** 3 copies, on 2 different media, with 1 offsite. Keep an **offline or immutable** copy to survive ransomware.
- **Snapshots** are quick rollback points, not a replacement for backups.

## Testing the plan

| Test | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Tabletop exercise** | Team talks through the plan; no systems touched |
| **Walkthrough / checklist** | Review steps and contacts |
| **Parallel test** | DR systems run alongside production |
| **Full interruption / failover** | Production actually fails over. Most realistic, highest risk |

Test regularly, at least annually and after major changes. Capture lessons in an **after-action report**.

> **Exam tip:** RP**O** = how much data you can lose (**O**ops, data gone). RT**O** = how long you can be down (**T**ime). "Talk through it" → tabletop.
